About the Indian Constitution

The Indian Constitution is the supreme law of India and serves as the foundation of the country's democratic system. It was adopted on 26 November 1949 by the Constituent Assembly and came into effect on 26 January 1950, a day celebrated every year as Republic Day. The Constitution establishes the structure and powers of the Government of India and defines the relationship between the central government, state governments, and citizens. It guarantees Fundamental Rights such as equality, freedom, and protection from discrimination while encouraging citizens to perform their Fundamental Duties. The Constitution is based on the principles of Justice, Liberty, Equality, and Fraternity, ensuring equal opportunities and protection under the law for every citizen. It also provides Directive Principles of State Policy to guide the government in promoting social and economic welfare. Drafted under the leadership of Dr. B. R. Ambedkar, the Constitution is one of the longest written constitutions in the world. It has been amended several times to meet the changing needs of society while preserving the values of democracy, unity, and national integrity. Today, the Indian Constitution continues to protect the rights of citizens, strengthen democratic institutions, and promote peace, justice, and development across the country.


Dr.B.R.Ambedkar

Dr. B. R. Ambedkar, also known as Bhimrao Ramji Ambedkar, is widely regarded as the Father of the Indian Constitution for his remarkable contribution to the drafting of India's Constitution. He served as the Chairman of the Drafting Committee and played a leading role in preparing a Constitution that guarantees justice, equality, liberty, and fundamental rights for all citizens. Born on 14 April 1891 in Mhow, Madhya Pradesh, Dr. Ambedkar overcame many social challenges through his dedication to education and hard work. He earned degrees from prestigious institutions such as Columbia University in the United States and the London School of Economics in the United Kingdom. As a lawyer, economist, social reformer, and political leader, he fought against social discrimination and worked tirelessly for the rights of marginalized communities. Dr. Ambedkar believed that education, equality, and justice were essential for building a strong and democratic nation. His leadership helped create a Constitution that protects the rights of every citizen and promotes unity, democracy, and social justice. His vision and values continue to inspire millions of people across India, and his birthday is celebrated every year as Ambedkar Jayanti in recognition of his lasting contribution to the nation.


Preamble of the Indian Constitution

The Preamble introduces the basic values and objectives of the Constitution.It declares India to be a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic. The Preamble promises Justice, Liberty, Equality, and Fraternity to all citizens and reflects the ideals of the nation.

  • Justice
  • Liberty
  • Equality
  • Fraternity

Fundamental Duties

Fundamental Duties remind every citizen of their responsibilities towards the nation and encourage them to contribute to the country's progress and development. These duties were added to the Indian Constitution by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976 to promote discipline, patriotism, and national unity. Every citizen should respect the Constitution, the National Flag, and the National Anthem while following the laws of the country. Citizens should protect the sovereignty, unity, and integrity of India and work together to maintain peace and harmony among people of different religions, cultures, and languages. They should safeguard public property, protect forests, rivers, lakes, and wildlife, and help preserve the environment for future generations. Fundamental Duties also encourage citizens to develop scientific thinking, value education, protect the country's rich cultural heritage, and strive for excellence in every field. By performing these duties sincerely, every citizen can help build a stronger, more united, and prosperous India.

  1. Respect the Constitution.
  2. Protect national unity.
  3. Safeguard public property.
  4. Protect the environment.
  5. Promote harmony among citizens.

Fundamental Rights

Fundamental Rights are the basic rights guaranteed to every citizen by the Constitution of India. These rights protect individual freedom, ensure equal treatment under the law, and help people live with dignity and self-respect. They are essential for the development of a democratic society and safeguard citizens from discrimination and unfair treatment. The Constitution guarantees important rights such as the Right to Equality, Right to Freedom, Right against Exploitation, Right to Freedom of Religion, Cultural and Educational Rights, and the Right to Constitutional Remedies. These rights allow citizens to express their opinions freely, practice any religion of their choice, receive equal opportunities, and seek justice when their rights are violated. Fundamental Rights promote justice, liberty, equality, and fraternity, which are the core values of the Indian Constitution. They help maintain peace, protect human dignity, and encourage every citizen to actively participate in the democratic process while respecting the rights and freedoms of others.

  1. Right to Equality
  2. Right to Freedom
  3. Right against Exploitation
  4. Right to Freedom of Religion
  5. Cultural and Educational Rights
  6. Right to Constitutional Remedies

Parliament of India

The Parliament of India is the highest legislative body of the country and plays a vital role in the functioning of India's democratic system. It consists of the President of India, the Lok Sabha (House of the People), and the Rajya Sabha (Council of States). The Parliament is responsible for making and passing laws, discussing important national issues, approving the Union Budget, and ensuring that the government works according to the Constitution. Members of Parliament represent the people from different states and union territories, giving citizens a voice in the country's decision-making process. Parliament debates policies, examines government actions, and introduces new laws that promote the welfare and development of the nation. It also has the power to amend the Constitution when necessary and to hold the government accountable for its decisions. Through discussions, debates, and legislation, the Parliament helps protect democracy, maintain national unity, and promote justice, equality, and the overall progress of India.

National Emblem of India



National Emblem of India

The National Emblem of India is the official symbol of the Republic of India and represents the country's strength, unity, and rich cultural heritage. It was adopted on 26 January 1950 from the Lion Capital of Ashoka located at Sarnath in Uttar Pradesh. The emblem features four Asiatic lions standing back to back, although only three are visible in the two-dimensional representation. Below the lions is a circular base that includes the figures of a horse, a bull, and the Dharma Chakra (Ashoka Chakra). The lions symbolize power, courage, confidence, and pride, while the Ashoka Chakra represents truth, righteousness, and continuous progress. Beneath the emblem is the national motto "Satyameva Jayate", written in the Devanagari script, which means "Truth Alone Triumphs." This motto is taken from the ancient Indian scripture, the Mundaka Upanishad, and reflects the importance of honesty and truth in society. The National Emblem appears on government documents, Indian currency, passports, official seals, and public institutions, serving as a symbol of the sovereignty, integrity, and democratic values of India.
