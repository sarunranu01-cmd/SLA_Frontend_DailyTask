02

Job change

Disappear vs shrink vs transform

Disappear — mostly tasks, not whole professions as a fact

SCENARIO Heavy automation of boilerplate, template UIs, and copy-paste integrations. Occupations rarely vanish overnight; tasks inside them can. Claiming “the developer disappears” is not this exhibition’s claim.

Shrink — fewer seats for the same title

If one person plus tools can cover what three juniors did, organizations may hire fewer people for routine CRUD, L1, and checkbox QA. Headcount is a management choice, not a law of physics.

Transform — same badge, different day

Remaining engineers, testers, and analysts could become directors, reviewers, integrators, and incident owners. The job title can stay while the skill mix rotates.

08

Ripples

Economic and social effects

Possible effects—not measured outcomes. Benefits and risks can arrive together.

Employment & salaries

Some roles could pay more if scarce (evaluation, security). Entry wages could compress if supply of graduates meets fewer seats.

Graduates & education

Degrees may matter less than demonstrated specification and review. Internships could become the bottleneck.

Firms & productivity

Smaller teams might ship more. Inference cost, liability, and brand risk could offset cheap drafts.

Inequality & access

Those with tools, English, and capital may pull away from those without. Public access is a political choice.

Skill & atrophy

Practice is how juniors become seniors. If practice work is fully automated, craft can thin.

Accountability & mental load

Reviewing endless generated work can exhaust. Blame may concentrate on fewer named humans.

09

Balance

Positive vs negative possibilities

Potential benefits

  • Higher productivity
  • Lower software costs for some products
  • Faster iteration
  • Small teams building large surfaces
  • Wider access to drafting tools
  • New kinds of work (eval, audit, safety)

Potential risks

  • Entry-level shortage
  • Unequal access
  • Skill atrophy
  • Economic inequality
  • Overdependence on vendors
  • Accountability gaps and power concentration

10

Emergence

Jobs that may emerge

Examples of complementary roles that could appear if generation is cheap and mistakes are expensive.

AI evaluator

Designs tests that tell a team when a model is good enough—and when it is not.

AI workflow designer

Decides which steps to automate and which exceptions stay human.

AI auditor

Independent review of data, claims, and controls.

AI safety specialist

Reduces misuse and high-impact failure modes in agents and tools.

AI governance specialist

Turns policy into logs, roles, and stop-buttons.

AI cybersecurity defender

Defends models, prompts, and generated supply chains.

Human–AI interaction designer

Makes uncertainty visible instead of fake confidence.

Domain + AI specialist

Healthcare, finance, climate, education: the error is domain-shaped.

AI compliance specialist

Maps systems to rules that already exist or may arrive.

AI systems supervisor

Owns the plant: cost, quality, incidents.
